# S5.2a WhatsApp action attribution and automatic replies

Status: built
Branch: slice/S5.2a-whatsapp-action-foundation
Size: M

## Goal

The API can send durable automatic replies and record a booking initiated by a linked WhatsApp message without attributing it to staff.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Trusted initiator.** Only S5.1's credential-verified callback can produce a WhatsApp action context, from a persisted direct inbound message and its clinic/account/contact/patient link. Submitted patient/clinic ids and patient.phone never establish identity. Unknown senders, wrong sessions, groups and outgoing echoes cannot obtain it. Booking requires the linked patient to be active.
2. **Truthful records.** Staff writes retain their actor. WhatsApp booking audit/timeline rows reference the initiating message, whose contact identifies the sender. Reads display “Patient via WhatsApp” as channel provenance. No invented user or borrowed staff identity. Staff routes retain their roles.
3. **Shared booking rules.** Existing appointment/follow-up/recall booking services accept a staff actor or trusted WhatsApp context. The latter can book only for its linked patient and an explicitly selected source belonging to that patient. Reuse scheduling, active-patient, overlap and transaction rules. All booking paths and staff appointment reschedule/cancel/status writes lock the patient before appointment/source rows, so S5.2b's final existing-visit check serializes with those writes. S5.2b adds contact/session locks after the patient. Staff permissions and status rules remain unchanged; no other patient-driven operation becomes available. Internal tests exercise this contract before the dialogue exists.
4. **Durable replies.** Persist an automatic reply with the incoming action, uniquely caused by that message. Dispatch after commit through `MessagingProvider`, in persisted order with at most one in-flight send per contact. Claim queued replies once; restarts resume queued replies but never redispatch sending/accepted/failed/unknown attempts. Before dispatch recheck account/link, allowlist, patient eligibility and any workflow guard. Mark an obsolete queued reply `superseded`; S5.2b supplies menu-version/expiry/pause guards. Ambiguous outcomes remain visible and never rebook or roll back a committed visit. Replayed callbacks cannot repeat the action/reply. Preserve staff-send request-id behavior; no values/provider errors in logs or patient locks held across provider calls.
5. **Compatible reads.** Inbox/history distinguish staff, incoming patient and automatic messages, including queued/unconfirmed states. Timeline handles both actors. Queries/FKs are clinic-scoped; staff request bodies reject forged actor fields. Existing reads/workflows still work after migrating old rows.
6. **Abandoned claims do not strand the queue.** Persist claim ownership and a bounded send deadline. Use an exclusive per-contact process/session fence across a bounded provider call; only after the old owner is proven inactive may another dispatcher mark a crash-left sending attempt unknown and advance to the next queued reply. Never resend the abandoned attempt. A deadline alone must not permit overlapping live senders. Stuck live owners are reported without busy waiting, and other contacts continue; S5.2b's STATUS must be able to reply after crash recovery. Finalization is conditional on the claim owner.

## Changes

- Data/migration: next Alembic revision after S5.1 (verify head). On `audit_event` and `timeline_event`, add `actor_kind` (`staff`/`whatsapp`, backfill staff) and nullable `actor_message_id`; make `actor_user_id` nullable. Checks require exactly the appropriate actor. Retain staff FKs; add composite clinic FKs to the initiating message. On S5.1 `message`, add unique `(id,clinic_id)`, `origin` (`staff`/`provider`/`automation`, backfill from direction), nullable `caused_by_message_id` with same-clinic FK and unique automatic cause, outbound `queued`/`superseded` states, and an internal identity `dispatch_order` with clinic/contact/order index. Revise direction/state/creator checks so automatic outbound rows have an inbound cause and no staff creator; only queued attempts may become superseded. Services verify the cause's direction/contact/account. No existing history is deleted.
- Claim metadata: nullable internal `dispatch_owner` UUID and `dispatch_deadline_at` on `message`, with paired-value checks and a sending-claim lookup index. Historical S5.1 sending rows without an owner remain unconfirmed and are never redispatched; new queued-dispatch claims require ownership. A PostgreSQL session-level advisory lock on an independent dispatcher connection may supply the per-contact fence; no business row lock or transaction stays open over the provider call. Fence loss stops that owner's further dispatch/finalization.
- Services/API: explicit actor types at booking/audit/timeline seams; no new endpoint, role or staff-auth bypass. Timeline actor response gains `kind`, with nullable staff `id` and existing display `name`. Message response gains `origin` and queued/superseded states; provenance/order ids stay internal. Regenerate OpenAPI/types. Automated-send audits use the initiating message context; superseded displays as not sent.
- Runtime/UI: an in-process API dispatcher resumes queued replies with independent DB sessions, serialized claims and abandoned-owner recovery; no extra deployment service/dependency. Include a pre-claim workflow/rendering step so S5.2b can refresh queued result bodies before freezing them in sending state. Recheck guards at claim; after claim no body rewrite or retry is allowed. Render automatic actors/states in messaging/timeline views. No patient dialogue or automatic booking trigger yet.

## Out of scope

Booking menus/offers (S5.2b), patient registration/login, other patient-driven writes, staff role changes, new providers/accounts, retries of uncertain sends and the deferred booking-ownership refactor.

## Decisions and risks

Approved 2026-11-03 as the first part of S5.2, including the listed existing-table and actor-contract changes. Build S5.1 first and reconcile its actual schema. Use inbound-message provenance instead of a system staff user. Staff-sent invitations retain the staff actor; the patient's confirmation supplies the booking actor. An explicitly linked test number is sufficient only for this synthetic demo. External send and DB commit cannot be atomic: a crash after claiming can leave an uncertain result requiring inspection.

Review corrections authorised 2026-11-04 include patient-first locks for all appointment mutations, dispatch claim metadata/fencing and pre-send result rendering. Recovery marks an abandoned attempt unknown; it does not retry it or promise provider delivery ordering.

### Decisions made

- Use a local OS process fence inherited by the bounded send child. This survives a dispatcher
  crash while its child is still active. All API processes must share the same host, user and
  temporary directory; separate hosts or isolated containers are not supported by this demo.
- Share that fence with staff sends. A staff attempt made while the fence is busy is saved as
  failed; replay returns the same attempt. A PostgreSQL advisory gate serializes patient status
  changes with sends without holding a patient row lock over a provider call.
- Use a short-lived, process-signed callback capability for internal WhatsApp actors. The
  callback secret, configured clinic/account/allowlist and stored inbound message establish
  the actor; no patient ID or staff ID is accepted from an HTTP body.
- Do not downgrade WhatsApp-attributed rows by inventing a staff actor. The migration downgrade
  fails if such rows remain, rather than deleting their provenance.

### Unsure points

- Review the local process-fence and spawned-provider approach, especially its single-host
  assumption and process termination behavior. It is not a distributed deployment design.
- Review whether “failed” is sufficiently clear when a staff send finds the contact busy.
- Live OpenWA pairing, real provider send/reply, restart and licence behavior remain unverified
  under the S5.1 deferred follow-up; all proof here uses synthetic data and a fake provider.
