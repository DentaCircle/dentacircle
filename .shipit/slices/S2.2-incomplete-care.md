# S2.2 Incomplete care

Status: built
Branch: slice/S2.2-incomplete-care
Size: M (one new table, one hook in the visit-complete path, one read route, one line on the care page)

## Goal

When a clinician completes a visit and the patient still has unfinished care items, the patient gets one open follow-up (or the existing one is updated), so unfinished work lands in the single coordinator queue.

## Acceptance criteria

### API

1. **Schema.** `alembic upgrade head` creates `follow_up`. `downgrade` to `0009` removes it. Upgrade, downgrade, upgrade works. The database refuses: a status outside `scheduled`, `contacted`, `booked`, `completed`, `cancelled`; a second open follow-up (`scheduled`, `contacted` or `booked`) for one patient; and a patient or visit of another clinic. A new follow-up is allowed when the earlier ones are `completed` or `cancelled`.
2. **Create.** When a visit moves to `completed` and its patient has at least one care item that is `planned` or `started`, one `follow_up` row is created in the same transaction: status `scheduled`, `due_on` = the clinic-local date of completion plus 7 days, `appointment_id` = the completed visit, `patient_id` from the visit. The route response is unchanged.
3. **Nothing open, nothing created.** No plans, or only `completed` and `cancelled` items: the visit completes and no follow-up is written.
4. **Whole patient, not the session.** Open items count across all of the patient's plans, including items never listed in a care session and visits with no session at all.
5. **Update, not a second row.** If the patient already has an open follow-up (any of the three open statuses), no row is added. Its `appointment_id` becomes the completed visit. Its status and `due_on` are not changed. A `completed` or `cancelled` follow-up does not count, so a new one is created.
6. **Inactive patient.** A visit of a patient who is now `inactive` still completes and writes no follow-up.
7. **Only completion triggers it.** `arrived`, `in_progress`, `no_show`, cancel, reschedule, saving a care session, and moving a care item write no follow-up.
8. **All or nothing.** If the follow-up write or its audit write fails, the visit stays `in_progress` and no row or audit event is saved.
9. **Audit.** Create writes `follow_up` / `create` / `changed_fields: []`. Update writes `follow_up` / `update` / `["appointment_id"]`. The existing `appointment` / `status_change` row is still written. The actor is the user who completed the visit. No names, titles or dates are stored in audit rows.
10. **No double follow-up.** Two visits of one patient completed at once give one follow-up, not an error. The patient row is locked after the visit row, and the partial unique index is the backstop.
11. **Read.** `GET /patients/{id}/follow-up` returns 200 `{follow_up: {id, patient_id, appointment_id, status, due_on, created_at}}` for the patient's open follow-up, or `{follow_up: null}`. `due_on` is a date with no time. A closed follow-up is not returned.
12. **Who may do what.** `GET`: `clinician`, `receptionist`, `clinic_admin`. `inventory_admin` gets 403. No session gets 401. Order of checks: 401, role, 404 (unknown or other-clinic patient). The status route's roles are unchanged.
13. **Tenancy.** Clinic A gets 404 on clinic B's patient. Clinic A completing its visit never reads or writes clinic B's follow-ups.
14. **Contract and checks.** The route-walk test passes. `openapi.json` and `schema.d.ts` are regenerated. `make check` and `make web-build` pass.

### Web

15. **Care page line.** `/patients/[id]/care` shows "Follow-up due 24 Oct 2026" with a status badge (Scheduled, Contacted or Booked) above the plans, for the three roles. With no follow-up it shows nothing. The date is the stored `due_on` shown as written, with no timezone shift.
16. **Failure states.** If the follow-up cannot be loaded, the plans still show, with "Follow-up could not be loaded." `inventory_admin` still sees "You do not have access." No button and no edit: this slice only shows the follow-up.

The item test walks all four item statuses. The role test walks all four roles on the GET and checks the table is unchanged. Before handing over, the builder breaks these once on purpose (the open-item check, the clinic scope, the "update not insert" branch, the patient lock, the all-or-nothing rollback), sees a test go red, and restores it. The result goes in the review packet. Web tests assert on what the user sees.

## Changes

- Data (`clinic_id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` as on other tables, UUID ids):
  - `follow_up`: `id`, `clinic_id`, `patient_id`, `appointment_id` (the latest visit that found unfinished care), `status` (text, check in the five stored statuses, default `scheduled`), `due_on` (date, not null). Unique `(id, clinic_id)`. Composite FKs `(patient_id, clinic_id)` to `patient` and `(appointment_id, clinic_id)` to `appointment`. Partial unique index on `(clinic_id, patient_id)` where status is `scheduled`, `contacted` or `booked`. Index `(clinic_id, status, due_on)` for the S2.4 queue.
  - No reason column, no care item count, no notes. S2.4 can show the unfinished items by reading them live, so nothing goes stale.
- Migration `0010`: one new table. **Additive only.** No existing table or column changes.
- API (`clinic_id` from the session):

  | Route | Roles | Result |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `GET /patients/{id}/follow-up` | clinician, receptionist, clinic_admin | 200, 401, 403, 404 |

  `POST /appointments/{id}/status` keeps its request and response. On `completed` it calls the follow-up service in the same transaction.
- Code, in the existing layers: `models/follow_up.py`; `domain/follow_up.py` (statuses, open statuses, default 7 days, roles, the "needs follow-up" rule as a pure function); `repositories/follow_up_repository.py`; `services/follow_up_service.py` (detect, create or update, read). `appointment_service.change_status` calls it after the status is set, so the route stays thin. `api/follow_ups.py` for the read route.
- UI: one block in `features/care/` and a loader call in `app/(app)/patients/[id]/care/page.tsx`. No nav change.
- No new dependency.

## Out of scope

- Manual create, the clinician-set due date, and any change of follow-up status (contacted, booked, completed, cancelled). That is S2.3 and S2.4.
- The coordinator queue, due and overdue, and booking from the queue (S2.4). `due_on` is stored here and not interpreted.
- Closing a follow-up when care finishes. A follow-up stays open until a coordinator closes it in S2.4.
- Recalls (S2.5), the timeline (S2.6), messages and reminders.
- Follow-ups for visits that were completed before this slice. No backfill.
- Checking whether the patient already has a future visit booked.
- Any change to care items, care sessions, or the visit rules.

## Decisions and risks

The roadmap needs one change, and the rest are choices to confirm (recommendation first):

- **S2.2 owns the `follow_up` table, and S2.3 shrinks (recommended).** No follow-up model exists yet, so this slice must create one. The roadmap lists "auto-create when care is unfinished" under both S2.2 and S2.3. I suggest S2.3 becomes "manual create, plus the clinician-set due date on completion", with the incomplete-care part done here. If you agree, I update ROADMAP.md. Alternative: put the table in S2.3 and make S2.2 a pure detection function with no storage. That fails the roadmap's "creates or updates the follow-up" and gives nothing to see.
- **Trigger is the visit completing (recommended).** This follows the S2.1b decision in PRODUCT.md. Tradeoff: a visit that never completes never creates one, and there is no undo on Complete.
- **Open items across the whole patient, with or without a session (recommended).** A consultation visit that only plans a treatment has no session but still needs a call back. Tradeoff: a patient with an old planned item gets a follow-up after any visit, such as a routine check. Alternative: only when a session exists for the visit. Quieter, but it hides the consultation case in the demo thread.
- **Default due date is completion date plus 7 days, fixed in code.** It is a guess. The spec lists follow-up defaults under settings, but there is no settings screen. Tradeoff: the clinic cannot change it without code. Alternative: due the same day. S2.3 lets the clinician set the date.
- **An existing open follow-up keeps its status and due date.** Only the visit link moves. We never push a coordinator's date. Tradeoff: a `booked` follow-up stays `booked` even if the new visit found more unfinished work. S2.4 decides what booking means.
- **Inactive patients get no follow-up.** Inactive means retired, so they should not appear on a queue. Tradeoff: open care of a retired patient is dropped silently.

Risks:

- A follow-up is not closed when the care is later finished. It can sit in the queue after the work is done until S2.4 gives the coordinator a way to close it. Add to `.shipit/open.md` when built if S2.4 does not cover it.
- The complete route now writes two more rows. It already locks the visit. It will also lock the patient row, always after the visit row.
- The branch you are on is `slice/S2.1b-care-sessions`, which is sealed but not yet merged. Branch S2.2 from it or from `main` once that merges.

### Decisions the brief left open

- No `updated_at`. No other table has one, and this slice never edits a row except `appointment_id`.
- The path parameter is `patient_id`, matching the other patient routes.
- `due_on` uses the clock passed into `change_status`, not the visit start. A completion at 23:30 UTC is the next clinic day.
- The patient row is locked on every completion, before the open-item check, and always after the visit row.

### Unsure points

- A follow-up is not closed when the care is later finished. S2.4 marks contacted and books; it does not close one because the items are done. Noted in `.shipit/open.md`.
- Two completes at once depend on the patient lock plus the partial unique index. The lock is what makes the second transaction update instead of insert.

### Break it once
