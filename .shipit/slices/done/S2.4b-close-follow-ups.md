# S2.4b Close follow-ups

Status: done
Branch: slice/S2.4b-close-follow-ups
Size: M (one new route, two service hooks, one button, no migration)

## Goal

A follow-up closes when its booked visit is completed, returns to the Due view when that visit is cancelled or missed, and a coordinator can cancel any open follow-up from the queue.

## Acceptance criteria

### Completing the booked visit

1. **Completion closes the follow-up.** `POST /appointments/{id}/status` with `completed`, where the visit is the `booked_appointment_id` of a `booked` follow-up, sets that follow-up to `completed`. Same transaction as the visit change. This runs before S2.2, so the open-follow-up slot is free when S2.2 looks.
2. **S2.2 and S2.3 then run as written.** After the close, an active patient with a `planned` or `started` item gets a new `scheduled` follow-up (`due_on` = completion day + 7, `appointment_id` = this visit). A clinician `follow_up_due_on` creates a new one with that date. No open item and no date: the patient has no open follow-up.
3. **Only the booked visit closes it.** Completing any other visit for the patient leaves a `booked` follow-up as S2.2 leaves it today (status kept, `appointment_id` moves). A follow-up that is `scheduled` or `contacted` is never closed by a completion. A follow-up already `completed` or `cancelled` is not touched.
4. **Inactive patient.** The visit still completes and the follow-up still closes (a done visit is a fact). No new follow-up is written, as in S2.2.

### Booked visit cancelled or missed

5. **Cancel releases the follow-up.** `POST /appointments/{id}/cancel` on the booked visit of a `booked` follow-up sets that follow-up to `contacted`. Same transaction. `due_on` and `booked_appointment_id` are kept. The row is in the Due view again when `due_on` is today or earlier. Cancelling any other visit leaves follow-ups alone.
6. **No-show releases it too.** `POST /appointments/{id}/status` with `no_show` does the same as 5.
7. **Reschedule changes nothing.** The same visit row moves, so the follow-up stays `booked`.

### Cancel from the queue

8. **Route.** `POST /follow-ups/{id}/cancel` (no body) moves a `scheduled`, `contacted` or `booked` follow-up to `cancelled` and returns 204. A closed row gives 409 `invalid_status` and changes nothing. There is no undo and no reason. Cancelling a `booked` row does not touch the visit.
9. **Inactive patient.** Gives 409 `patient_inactive` and writes nothing, as mark contacted does.
10. **A new follow-up is possible afterwards.** After 1, 5 to 6 or 8, the patient has no open row (if none was created by 2), so `POST /patients/{id}/follow-up` works again.

### Rules for all of the above

11. **Audit.** Each status move writes `follow_up` / `status_change` / `["status"]`, same transaction, actor is the user who acted. No values are stored. If an audit write fails, the whole request is rolled back, including the visit change.
12. **Roles and order of checks.** The cancel route allows `clinician`, `receptionist`, `clinic_admin`. `inventory_admin` gets 403, no session 401. Order: 401, role, 404 (unknown or other-clinic follow-up), 409. Roles on the appointment routes are unchanged.
13. **Concurrency.** Two cancels at once on one follow-up: one 204, one 409. A cancel racing the booked visit's completion ends with the follow-up in exactly one final state and no deadlock. Lock order stays visit, then patient, then follow-up.
14. **Existing behaviour holds.** S2.2, S2.3, S2.4 and S1.x tests pass. Tests that expected a booked follow-up to stay open after its own visit completed (none found so far) are updated and listed in the review packet. The route-walk test passes. `openapi.json` and `schema.d.ts` are regenerated. `make check` and `make web-build` pass.

### Web

15. **Cancel button.** Each row on `/follow-ups` has "Cancel follow-up". It asks for a confirmation ("Cancel this follow-up? This cannot be undone.") with a confirm and a keep action. On success the row is gone and "Follow-up cancelled." shows. A 409 refreshes and shows "This follow-up has already changed." A 403 shows "You do not have access."
16. **Empty copy and notice.** The notice type in `links.ts` gains `cancelled`. Nothing else on the page changes.

### Proof that it works

17. **Tests that can fail.** Each rule in 1 to 13 has a test that fails if the rule is removed. Before handing over, the builder breaks these once on purpose (the booked-visit match, the order against S2.2, the release on cancel, the status guard on cancel route), sees a test go red, restores it, and records it in the review packet.
18. **Screenshots** of the real app on synthetic data, in `.shipit/proof/S2.4b/` with an `index.md` and the commit hash:

## Out of scope

- Closing a `scheduled` or `contacted` follow-up when care is later finished. S2.2 decided that a coordinator closes it, and Cancel is now how.
- A reason for cancelling, notes, contact history, or an undo.
- A "complete" button on the queue. Completion happens only through the booked visit.
- Showing the booked visit's date in the queue.
- Recalls (S2.5), the timeline (S2.6), dashboard counts (S6.1), role-based nav (S7.1).
- Changing what happens to a visit when its follow-up is cancelled. The visit stays.

## Decisions and risks

- **Release on cancel and no-show (recommended, needs your call).** The roadmap line names only completion and cancel. Without a release, a patient whose booked visit is cancelled or missed has a `booked` follow-up that is hidden from the Due view, and nobody sees them again unless someone opens All open. That breaks the core loop silently. The release is a few lines in two functions. Tradeoff: it widens the slice and puts a follow-up write inside the appointment cancel and no-show paths. Alternative: leave it out, and the coordinator cancels the stale row and makes a manual follow-up (the date and contacted state are lost). If you decline, remove criteria 5 and 6 and the matching test and screenshot. I would then change the ROADMAP.md wording after your decision.
- **Release goes to `contacted`, not `scheduled`.** The coordinator did reach the patient. Tradeoff: if the patient was booked straight from `scheduled`, the row now says Contacted. The booking is the contact. Alternative: `scheduled`.
- **Match by `booked_appointment_id` and status `booked`.** It is exact. Tradeoff: a follow-up marked booked some other way (only tests do that) is not closed. Rows booked before migration `0012` would have no visit id, but the column came with the booking route, so none exist.
- **Cancel from all three open statuses.** The coordinator needs a way out of a stale `scheduled` row too. Tradeoff: cancelling a `booked` row leaves the visit on the calendar. That is stated on the confirmation only if you want it; I have not added extra text.
- **Cancel returns 204.** The row leaves the list, and the list item shape has no cancelled status. Tradeoff: it differs from contacted (200 with the item). Alternative: widen the item status type to include `cancelled`.
- **Lock order.** Completion and release take the visit lock first, then the patient, then the follow-up. The cancel route takes patient, then follow-up and never the visit, so no cycle. `change_status` and `cancel_appointment` are edited here and S2.5 would edit `change_status` again. Merge S2.4b first.
- **Branch.** The working branch is still `slice/S2.4-coordinator-queue`. This slice needs its own branch from the sealed S2.4 work.

### Decisions made

- `complete_booked_follow_up` does not lock the patient. `record_incomplete_care` already holds that lock, then locks the follow-up. A second patient lock would make the existing two-completes test stop overlapping. `release_booked_follow_up` does lock the patient, because cancel and no-show did not.
- The close is flushed before a new follow-up is inserted, so the one-open-row rule sees the old row as closed.
- Criterion 10 after a release: the row is `contacted`, which is still open, so `POST /patients/{id}/follow-up` is refused. A new follow-up works after a completion that writes no replacement, and after cancel from the queue.
- Confirm actions are "Yes, cancel" and "Keep", the same words as cancelling a visit. The question text is the one in the brief.
- A 409 uses the existing `changed` notice ("This follow-up has already changed.") and refreshes the list.

### Unsure points

- The question sits in the status cell, so on a narrow pane it wraps inside the word "follow-up".
- Cancelling a booked row does not say that the visit stays on the calendar. The brief left that sentence out.

### Checklist

- Tenancy: the new read uses `scoped_select`. A follow-up from another clinic is 404.
- Roles: the cancel route declares clinician, receptionist, clinic_admin. Inventory is 403. The queue page already hides the list from that role.
- No migration. Status moves share the request transaction. A failed follow-up audit rolls the visit change back.
- Rules live in the service. Invalid status and an inactive patient are rejected.
- Audit stores `follow_up` / `status_change` / `["status"]` and no values.
- Tests went red when the booked-visit match, the close-before-new-row order, the release on cancel, and the cancel status guard were each removed, then green again when restored.
- The queue test that said a booked row has no actions now expects Cancel follow-up. No existing test expected a booked follow-up to stay open after its own visit was completed.
